RideTrack: a mobile app for live tracking and QR ticketing

We are a team of four third-year undergraduates at the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT). As part of the IT3060 Human Computer Interaction module we are building RideTrack, an Android app that shows passengers where buses and trains are, when they will arrive, and lets them buy a QR ticket that a conductor or ticket inspector scans on board.

The app includes a dedicated transport authority section in which officers manage vehicles, routes, stops, schedules and fares, watch live operations, review incidents reported by staff, and publish service alerts that reach passengers' phones immediately. We have designed this section with officers in mind and would value your view on whether it reflects how your work is actually done.

    What it is

    An Android app for Sri Lankan public transport. A passenger first chooses bus or train; the whole app then shows only that type. Three user roles share one live database:

    RoleWhat they can do
    PassengerSearch routes, filter and sort trips, see stops and facilities, track a vehicle live on a map, book and pay (simulated), keep a QR ticket, cancel, view history, receive alerts.
    Conductor / inspectorSign in as staff, view the assigned journey, scan and validate QR tickets, see passenger counts, report incidents and delays, complete the journey.
    Transport authorityManage vehicles, routes, stops, schedules, fares and facilities; preview and publish changes; watch live operations; review incidents; publish alerts.

    Links between roles: (1) the passenger's QR ticket is scanned by the conductor; (2) a conductor's incident report reaches the authority; (3) an authority alert appears in passengers' notifications.

    Testing and releaseFunctional, integration and usability testing (5+ participants) to follow; Android APK release

    Technology and data

    • React Native with Expo (SDK 57), TypeScript, Google Maps; Firebase Firestore and Authentication for real-time data and role-based sign-in.
    • Payments are simulated; no card or bank details are collected.
    • All route, timetable and fare data in the app is currently sample data prepared by the team, not official data.
    • Access to the database is controlled by role-based security rules; passenger data is used only for tickets and alerts.
